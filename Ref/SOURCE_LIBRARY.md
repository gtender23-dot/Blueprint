## Important mechanics note — how these links resolve

blitzology is a Blogger site. Two kinds of URL were sent:

- **Direct article** — `/YYYY/MM/slug.html`. One specific post. Fetchable directly.
- **Label/index** — `/search/label/<Tag>`. A tag feed. **Blocked by robots.txt for the
  WebFetch tool** (Blogger disallows `/search/` for crawlers), so these can only be
  read through the browser (Claude-in-Chrome), not WebFetch.

Critical quirk found during the sweep: **a label page renders the single most-recent
post carrying that tag, in full — not a list of links.** Because each post is tagged
with many labels, *different label URLs frequently resolve to the same article.* So the
~18 label links collapse to only a handful of distinct articles. Deduped below.

### A. Attacking protection / overload pressure  ← core protection-logic material

1. **Attacking Bonus Protection** — `/2020/06/attacking-bonus-protection.html`
   How a defense beats "bonus" protection (offense shifts RB to make it 4/2 across the
   center to pick up a 4-from-a-side overload). Direct article; also the post surfaced
   by labels **`1 High`** and **`2 Under 3 Deep`** (same article, three ways in).
   Concepts: bonus protection (4/2 vs 3/3), 4-from-a-side overload, hot throw, green-dog
   (rush-to-cover), quarters/2-Read backstop, 2-under-3-deep economics.

2. **Attacking 1/2 Slide** — surfaced by label **`Attacking 1/2 Slide`**
   The protection-vs-rush chess match spelled out: Big-on-Big (BOB) man protection and
   its Fan/Molly variants, half-slide zone protection (4-on-4), the "block inside rusher
   first" rule, "first uncovered OL starts the slide" rule, and Bluff / X adjustments that
   manipulate who the OL counts as a man vs. slide blocker.
   Concepts: BOB/Fan/Molly, half-slide, protection ID rules, slide direction, rush ordering.

### B. Non-traditional 4-man rush / front alignment to create rush mismatches

3. **Overload front, twist game, 2-Man coverage (2014 Eagles)** — label **`2 Man`**
   Odd-front overload putting both rush LBs same side on a twist; zero-tech nose pins the
   center so OG/OT are 2-for-2; coverage disguises 1-high then plays 2-Man with an X
   bracket. Concepts: overload front, DT/DE twist, center occupied → no help, disguise.

4. **Double-A mug, rush-LB A-gaps vs half-slide (2014 Eagles, Barwin)** — label **`Dime Personnel`**
   Double-A-gap mug forces 6 one-on-ones incl. the RB; using rush-LBs (not DL) as the
   A-gap threats stresses the offense's mug protection rules and isolates an elite rusher

5. **Align to Win — X-rusher package & front flips** — label **`Align to Win`**

   rushers; both DEs to one side; the "X" rusher package (Edge / Gilligan = guard-on-an-
   island) to align your best rusher anywhere with base personnel.
   Concepts: personnel≠spacing, alignment to force matchups, isolate best rusher, 2-way go.

### A2. Fire-zone / man pressure attacking protection (added 2026-08-05)  ← core protection material

13. **Cover 3 Fire Zone — half-slide broken by a Mike alignment shift** — label **`Cover 3 Fire Zone`**

    The post then walks **nine** distinct offensive protection answers (ignore it / throw hot /

    redirect problem, empty 5-man scat protection.

54. **Running out the clock / Quarterback kneel (numeric)** — American Football Database (Fandom)

    ✅ **Numeric anchor** for subsystem 5, fix B (used 2026-08-06). The victory-formation math:
    with the 40-sec play clock, a leading team can kneel on 1st/2nd/3rd and run off **~120 seconds

    holds stops the clock after a kneel, so that kneel burns just a few seconds. Viable "kneel it out"
    threshold ≈ the 2-minute warning with the ball + a lead + the opponent out of TOs. This is the
    source the timeout-aware kneel gate was calibrated to. Concepts: kneel seconds, play-clock drain,

    FG is worth **2.3 EP** net of the ensuing kickoff, a TD **6.3 EP**, and the 1st-down EP surface by
    field position (opp-20 ≈ 3.7 EP, own-5 ≈ −0.5). The framework that says short-yardage 4th downs on

    position value.

56. **NFL 4th-down decision model (conversion rates + go/punt EP)** — Malter Analytics
    `malteranalytics.github.io/nfl-4th-down/`
    ✅ **Numeric anchor** for subsystem 5, fix C (used 2026-08-06). Concrete break-even example: **4th-
    and-3 converts ~45%**, and from your own 42 the go is worth **+3.15 EP vs +1.65 for a punt** — a
    clear go. Goal-to-go conversion is lower than open-field. This set the target the raised go-rate
    curve was anchored to (midfield 4th-and-1 → strong go, 4th-and-3 → ~34% call near the 45% convert).
    Concepts: 4th-down conversion rate by distance, go-vs-punt EP, goal-to-go dropoff.

### Q. Fire-zone run fits (added 2026-08-05, batch 6)  ← pressure + run-fit

    pressure to pass-strength instead of the field. Great on how run fits adjust inside a pressure.
    Primary subsystem: 1 (pressure) + 3 (run fits). Concepts: fire-zone run fits, spill/box tracks,

## F. Offensive formations — alignment truth  (added 2026-08-18)

F1. **The Flexbone Offense: An In-Depth Guide** — Throw Deep Publishing,
    Michael Campbell, 2024-10-30 —
    `throwdeeppublishing.com/blogs/football-glossary/the-flexbone-offense-an-in-depth-guide`
    Read 2026-08-18 to settle an owner bug report ("in flexbone trips the RB is
    taking the handoff from a split-out position"). Two quotes decided it:
    - **Base (Double Slot):** *"two A-backs positioned 2x4 yards outside and
      behind tackles"* — so an A-back aligned just outside the tackle taking a
      handoff is CORRECT flexbone, not a bug. Our base row already matches.
    - **Trips:** *"takes one of the A backs from one side, and moves him to the
      opposite side lined up in space as a receiver off the line of scrimmage"*
      — ONE A-back travels and becomes a receiver; the other STAYS in his base
      alignment. Our authored row did neither: it put the traveller at x0.70
      (behind the right tackle, not "in space") and shoved the staying A-back
      out to x0.84, which is what made his ordinary carries look absurd.
    Also useful and not yet modelled: the A-back pitch relationship is
    *"3-4 yards away"* from the QB; the **rocket toss** pitches to an A-back in
    motion (so a wide man carrying on a toss is right); base looks expect
    pre-snap A-back motion, and good staffs counter it with counter-motion.

F2. **The Midline Option: The Complete Guide** — Throw Deep Publishing,
    AJ Forbes, 2023-07-02 —
    `throwdeeppublishing.com/blogs/football-glossary/the-midline-option-the-complete-guide`
    Read 2026-08-18. BUILT FROM THIS (concept `Midline Option`, resolver
    `option-midline`): *"an option play where the Quarterback reads an interior
    Defensive Lineman… The play hits downhill to a dive back hitting the A-gap
    or if the QB keeps the ball, through the B-gap."* Three engine consequences,
    all probe-pinned in `tools/option_probe.mjs`:
    - the read key is the first DL **on or outside the guard** (a 3-tech), NOT
      an edge defender — that is what separates midline from veer/triple;
    - **no pitch phase** — two outcomes, give or keep;
    - the keep is **downhill through the B-gap** behind an inserting wingback,
      where a veer keep bends to the perimeter.
    The read direction matched what the engine already did for triple and is
    now commented at the site: *"If the dive key crashes toward the FB, keep the
    ball… if the dive key expands or runs upfield, hand the ball off."*
    NOT yet modelled, logged as candidates: the **"Follow"** tag (the second
    wingback comes downhill as an extra lead blocker for the keep — Army/Navy/
    Air Force short yardage); midline's twirl motion as deliberate *eye candy*
    that pulls linebackers and safeties to the perimeter while the ball goes up
    the gut; and the source's stated weakness — the QB "taking a lot of hits"
    running into the teeth of the defense, which today falls out only indirectly
    from routing his keep inside.
    **Rate gap:** the source establishes midline as a COMPLEMENT to a veer-based
    option offense but gives no call frequency, so `MIDLINE_SHARE = 0.22` is a
    provisional band, not a sourced one.
